06 · The bigger picture

Does observation effort affect recorded richness?

Each dot is one state or territory. The horizontal axis shows observation records; the vertical axis shows distinct species recorded in all those records.

Similar record totals can reveal different richness: the Northern Territory records 348 species from about 181,000 records; Tasmania records 233 from about 202,000.

More recording can reveal more species, but this chart does not measure the total wild bird population or establish a causal effect.

08 · Conclusion

What the observations can tell us

Across 5.24 million retained records, the charts reveal uneven recording activity, differences in recorded species richness and distinct patterns for familiar birds. Queensland has the highest mean richness in equal samples of 100,000 records, at about 529 species. The Rainbow Lorikeet case study shows why state and seasonal rates can add context to a national total.

These findings are about submitted observations from January–August 2025. More observers, trips and accessible locations can all shape the patterns. Equal-record comparisons help with differences in effort, but they do not turn sighting records into a measure of wild bird abundance or complete annual coverage.
