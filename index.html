02 - Meet Australian birds

Records and recorded richness

Where are most bird observations recorded?

Each circle represents one state or territory. Circle area scales with its observation record count. Larger circles indicate more submitted records.

Queensland has the largest state total. About 1.47 million observation records were submitted. Queensland and New South Wales contribute 54% together.

More observers and trips can increase record counts. One record represents one sighting, regardless of individual count. Mapped state totals include 5,243,943 records. Six records have unknown states and are excluded.

Which states record more bird species?

Each state is compared using 100,000 observation records. Darker green indicates more distinct recorded species. Equal record counts account for differences in recording volume.

Queensland leads the equal-record comparison. On average, 529 species appear in 100,000 records. That exceeds New South Wales by 86.5 species.

Colours show averages across 200 samples per state. Each sample contains 100,000 randomly selected records. Records cannot repeat within the same sample. Hover for the middle 90% of sample results. Observer bias remains.

08 - Conclusion

What the observations can tell us

Across 5.24 million records, reporting activity varies across Australia. Recorded richness and familiar species show distinct patterns. Queensland leads the equal-record comparison at about 529 species. Each state comparison uses samples of 100,000 records.

These findings cover January–August 2025 observations. Observer visits and accessible locations shape reported patterns. Equal-record comparisons help account for differing recording effort. They do not measure abundance or complete annual coverage.
